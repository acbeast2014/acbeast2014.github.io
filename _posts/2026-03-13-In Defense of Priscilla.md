_Some thoughts I have on Priscilla(2023) submitted this to writers2writers_

Cultural moments are always obscenely manufactured, they worm themselves around, between kernels of social truths and revulsion. It’s only in hindsight that we can reevaluate the drips of collective desires the ‘industry’ has captured then weaponized. When Priscilla came out in 2023 my ma told me “why would I watch another movie about an abusive man” (se aprovecho de la morrita). I’m not here to argue against that point of view — in fact I agree — but we would be remiss to not engage in the conversation surrounding violence and patriarchy both inside and outside the film.

Coppola's fierce portrayal of the manipulation of Priscilla is obscured by her treatments in visual aesthetics. The soft colors and soft textures (demure, obviously) in the frames containing an isolated Priscilla are the product of the process of converting the silence and loneliness imbued in Graceland into the sheer sheets of purity. It’s Elvis that mechanizes the jouissance of Priscilla's desire, here again I find it imperative to situate the analysis of the emotional extraction in favor of Priscilla. It would be simple to mirror the machinery between the couple to that of Elvis and the record company (the capitalist), but these other networks of exploitation cannot exist without the alienation of Priscilla from herself and displacement of her libidinal investments. The ending frustrated me when I saw it, but it's clear to me that the gates have closed behind her.
	
The conversation surrounding the movie was a mirror to the machinery hidden within the movie, it then follows that the poster would display “Priscilla” in illustrious silky letters. As her eyeliner thickens and her gaze immobilizes, the opportunity for objectification grows, one which the industry clearly took. Less clear (if you don't pay attention) is the bubbling fumes of violence, a deeper internal violence that is obscured by the marketing in a way that negates the machinery in Coppola's vignettes. I don't blame my ma for assuming things based off of the ‘cultural moment’ but there is an enduring honesty in the assertiveness of Priscilla's emotions. They are not primitive or marks of naive youth, there is strength and concrétude in her love!
