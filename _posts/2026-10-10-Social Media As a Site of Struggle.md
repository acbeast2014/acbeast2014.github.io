_I gave this talk on October 10th at the 4S conference, thank you to all of the people who helped me with this of which this would not be possible without_

Resistance movements against technofascism have historically been attached to technological devices that are subject to immense cyber espionage. I aim to use materialist STS to discuss the nature of how communication and organizing within the United States can be reconsidered for a liberatory future. I highlight this particular geography because I would like to make the distinction between the coercive subjugating force of surveillance in the global South and the consensual entrapment[9] of control societies in the United States. The laboratories of technofascism[4] that satellite the global south serve to strengthen and embellish surveillance techniques used within the imperial centers of capitalism. While there is a relationship between the two, the trajectory of these material realities and subsequent policy decisions are different. Furthermore, I will define technofascism as the alliance between imperial state violence and the economic modern conglomerate of tech corporations. A critical aspect of this alliance are the constant negotiations that take place between the institutions and capital. Surveillance of communication within imperial centers is not totalizing nor overarching; instead it’s an unstable collaboration for the maintenance of an effective level of information. 

In the same way that most communication does not enter the social media valorisation process[6], technofascist enterprises must go to great lengths to surveil. My goal in this talk is to analyze the methods in which corporations surveil their users and situate those techniques within the larger technofascist narrative. This understanding will allow us to rupture from the big brotherisms[9] of modern popular conception, replacing a nebulous composition of a new mythical capitalism[11] with the makeup of workers' struggle. Finally, we can trace the historical connection between the struggle for workers in creating networks of autonomy and modern digital repression.  

To better understand the macro mechanisms of tech surveillance I will first discuss Meta, the largest proponent for digital communication within the technofascist conglomerate, with 3 billion individual users. What specifically differentiated Meta’s from other methods of communication was its use of algorithms to concentrate social behaviors into centralized digital masses. While the technofascist alliance weaponizes these tendencies, it's necessary to outline exactly where it is and is not capable of fully implementing its own techniques. Although Meta is “integrated with the US state–corporate surveillance complex”[1]  the government publicly relies on Section 702 FISA searches, NSLs (national security letters) and backdoor searches to acquire information from electronic communication service providers (ESPs). Section 702 of the Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Act (FISA) allows analysts to gather information on an individual using their phone number or email based on the condition that said individual are not US citizens and are suspected of having ties to terror. This is of course very regularly dismissed, searches without probable cause can be understood as backdoor searches. NSLs are similar but instead can be called upon in relation to a national security investigation, requiring less legal or institutional justification. In 2021 it was reported by the ODNI that government agencies completed roughly 2.9 million of these requests[1], of this number a significant portion of those requests were aimed at and further reciprocated by meta. While there is reason to speculate that these numbers are larger than they are presented in both ODNI reports and Meta transparency reports because of later congressional recertifications of section 702 FISA and discrepancies in the agencies using “different counting methods,”[1] this largely would not affect our understanding of the engineered mechanisms behind the relationship between state observation and digital communication.

Cases outside of Meta reaffirm that surveillance techniques are largely incomplete. In the case of the recent FBI investigation of the “north Texas Antifa cell,” supposedly encrypted and inaccessible Signal messages were read aloud in trial with a complete record of time stamps and their corresponding identifications[2]. Information that, notably, was acquired through a physical phone acquisition rather than sophisticated cyber espionage. Not only does this demonstrate the opaque nature of digital enclosures, in a way digital encryption can also be understood as a marketing technique. But it also demonstrates the government's inability to prevent actual physical demonstrations from happening. Here I continue to invoke the rhetoric of the Black Panther Party in the idea that resistance and manifestation is not reactive but it is rather the superstructure that reacts[10]. When considering these techniques critically they are still by their very nature a selective, targeted and reactive process, technofascist monitoring is not an infinite data reservoir. In the words of Jasbir Puar, “Networks of surveillance in this three-dimensional, vertical setup are not removed, abstract, or cohered, but viciously intimate”[7]. This alliance is another step in a series of techniques that can be seen penetrating the history of resistance, one that is interested in the targeting and technological spying of specific groups of interest. 

Here it is paramount to recover the relationship between the historical combating of surveillance and its modern monstrous descendant we workers are setting ourselves up against. These developments in technology are not one in sophistication but rather in the further obfuscation of the bourgeois gaze, replacing the physical instantiation of the capitalist class with mechanisms of control. An expansive surveillance network is only relevant combined with an effective use of intelligence apparatus, something the algorithm on its own cannot provide. This is the same intelligence apparatus that resistance movements have been combating since its inception in history, ranging from COINTELPRO to the effective calculations bureaucracy makes when walking up and down the assembly line. It is this specific point which ties the practice of surveillance to the productive forces of society; this is a practice born of a specific historical necessity and cannot be understood without it. That being the suppression of a collective movement capable of disrupting the accumulation and extraction of capital. 

This is why it is imperative to situate our ability to use digital platforms for the purpose of collective organizing within the context of the political economy. Social media platforms exist as an extension of capital; it is continuously trying to renegotiate our subjectivity into a formation that favors an atomised individual away from its laboral (and therefore class) context. How do we better understand our collective sociality so that we can redirect it and weaponize it at capital? Linda Hubers’s recent Tech Lash to Tech Fash article motivated me to look at labor and organizing as a source for collective autonomy. While that paper goes into the literal outline of a future workers workshop, I would like to engage further with the theoretical underpinnings of such an activity. 

To quote Romano Alquati, an Italian operaista from the late 20th century, our emancipatory usage of a medium  “ depends very much on education”[3]. The collective education and reproduction of class consciousness must occur from the position of labor. Alquati explains that class composition “begins in manufacture”[8]. The socialization of classes occurs within the process of production, and while manufacture destroys the previous subjectivity of the worker it can not necessarily control its new outcome. This is what makes the centrality of production so dangerous for capital, why its technology continues to atomize and substratify us into solitary confinement. The struggle in communicating on social media platforms is not one directed at subverting the gaze of bourgeois but rather in positing “Formation as more important, central and prioritised than Communication itself.”[3] To build our own successful countersubjectivity on the medium we must rebuild our collective understanding of what constitutes our class character; we do this by grounding our theorizing within labor and class itself. Within the actual means and grounds in which our society produces we can encapsulate the networks of autonomy with which we can struggle against digital substratification.  

To conclude, Alquati said “The fact is that up there, at the top, in Capitalism, nothing changes; nothing ever changes; everything has remained exactly the same for centuries now!”[3]  The goal of this talk was to situate a critique on communication within the context of the digital sphere away from the technological sublime. The wet dreams of the technocratic elite are hollow, our enemy is “global racial capitalism supercharged by the digital infrastructures of state violence.”[4]  While our methods must be precise the dialectic still rings true, “What the bourgeoisie therefore produces, above all, are its own grave-diggers.”[5]
