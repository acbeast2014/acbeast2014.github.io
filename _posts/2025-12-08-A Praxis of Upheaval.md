The question of the state has passed from generation to generation, creating a new formulation in each different instance. Traditionally, the capitalist state has been seen as something that must be immediately taken, destroyed and replaced by a new socialist state. In her essay _The Rhythms of the Pachakuti_, Raquel Gutierrez interrogates that exact tradition within Marxism by redefining the word emancipation. In its new meaning, Gutierrez gives us the ability to change emancipation into an ongoing manifestation of communal will. This essay is interested in how emancipation moves beyond capital and our immediate capitalist subjectivity by reshaping the terrain of struggle beyond the state while retaining the structure of Marxist analysis.

Gutierrez sets out by stating emancipation can not be but minimally understood as a “transitive (requiring an object) and reflexive (the subject acts on itself ) verb"[1].  A verb that constellates the understanding of the “common capacity to take action and to decide for and by themselves"[1]. An understanding that comes about only by putting into perspective the ways in which subordination to capital relates to our position as subjugated individuals, in other words, a subjugated class. It is through this inherent contradiction that “social emancipation as a growing and intermittent trajectory or itinerary arises from the persistent state of subjection and powerlessness that we bear"[1]. Here, it then becomes abundantly clear that Gutierrez’s key for the continual definition of emancipation, is a historical methodology. It is something that is always culminating into itself and must be constantly communally reflected on. Evidence of this can be found in the references to the inscription of "concrete political activity”[1] of various groups in Bolivia during the mid 2000’s. Gutierrez further uses this concept to show how emancipation shows itself as a manifestation of material relations, a strategy that is being acted out not an idea that is being “constructed from the dominant social position” [1].

This new meaning of emancipation allows for Gutierrez to break from the Marxist tradition. She writes “taking power is neither a necessary nor a sufficient condition to change the world”[1]. This marks a significant departure from the Marxist-Leninist tradition, which deems the state as something that must immediately be taken and retooled, an exercise Gutierrez names as “done from power” [1]. This is precisely what this new interpretation of emancipation is trying to move away from. In the analysis of the revolutionary movements of the 20th century, history teaches (from what it didn't) what it must look like to subdue the “web of relations of power and sclerotic exploitation”[1]. For there to actually be a significant breakage from the subjection of capital there must be a significant collective movement focused on maintaining the ability to intervene. This is something the state, as an organism dedicated in its nature to the control of the symbiotics of power, is not fully equipped to do. This is shown through the fact that historically the state has never occupied itself with its own dissolution. The reason the taking of state power becomes a non-necessity is then because a centralized megastate becomes a roadblock in the upkeep of “the autonomy and political capabilities achieved by society itself"[1]. Without that autonomy there can be no process in which we can untie and render the “knots that give power and capital strength”[1] useless.

With this new definition, the aim of revolutionary movement shifts from a list of “systematic objectives”[1] to a focus inwards into the process of emancipation. The purpose of mobilization becomes “concerning oneself with caring for and expanding the autonomous capacity to intervene in public affairs"[1].  There is a certain type of social reproduction that exists within the process of social emancipation that allows for the “persistence of the capacity to subvert”[1] in the political. This specific type of social reproduction must come from social emancipation itself as it “insists on its own multiple possibilities for creation and production"[1]. Therefore, social emancipation exists as a form of reproduction of revolutionary social relations. This solidifies the energies that help render the necessity for a state obsolete. The framework of social reproduction is also what allows for Gutierrez’s emphasis on “the point of view of the particular” [1], emancipation at its particular exists within the network of all micro instabilities, this does not mean that emancipation exists as a key to universality but rather that emancipation maintains and ensures the persistence of the communal will (or collective drive.) This framework also inquires as to the gendered expression of reproduction. Gutierrez’s choice of the words “caring for” is crucial, as traditional iterations of social reproduction have entirely dismissed the question of the woman at home (Federici). The new definition of emancipation allows for the reintroduction of an expansive femininity into the core of the movement, one that exists outside of the “the place that is externally assigned to us in the framework of subjection to capital" [1].

Gutierrez states that the struggle for the “social, economic, and political must be conceived as a separate and distinct trajectory from that of the party struggle" [1]. These struggles exist in a different space-time because the electoral movement is materially distinct from the emancipatory. Even though they affect each other it is relevant to situate “the problem of the subject of social emancipation in its just location"[1].  It is through the Marxist analysis that Gutierrez establishes the identity of emancipation, one that strives to move as a precursor and respirator to revolution. In other words the “effort to elucidate that route and to expand and desubjectivize the flow of social energy” [1] that erodes capitalist subjectivity exists still as a result of materialist analysis. Fundamentally, this still exists as a production of imminent class antagonisms. Gutierrez is reaching into the antagonism to find the tools for reflexive action, action that is accessible through the subjugation of capital. In the amplification of identities under subjugation there begets a difference amongst each cell’s relation to the state, which is another reason why this struggle occurs in a different space-time. There is a demarcation in which the way the state expresses power over its people and the way the expression of capital is largely uninterested in how it crushes your airways. It prefers to view itself as a frigid observer, a reluctant participant in its own creation. The progressive government is shown as the main enforcer of the space-time of the state, “so far as they reedit and reinforce certain relationships of power that have nothing to do with the assembly, the horizontal, the autonomous"[1].  Gutierrez also firmly critiques the reformist electoral movements of Latino America's history because they are especially interested in retooling and strengthening the imbalance of power that existed in the capitalist government. The answer still can not exist in the state, much less in the progressivist one.

All of this is in favor of saying that the way in which the redefinition of emancipation places itself beyond the capitalist subjectivity, is through introducing a method in which to reformulate social reproduction as a ground of struggle. This reformulation exists not as a stark rejection or retooling of materialism, but as its direct application in the 21st century. Gutierrez is not functioning as an anarchist, her analysis is not one primarily motivated by the decimation of hierarchy. This does not mean this is not a commonality, the elimination of hierarchy is still a goal to be met after all, it means instead that the fuel behind her analysis is a Marxist historical one. An analysis fueled by the antagonisms between classes and their results. 

Emancipation, then, becomes a liberatory positive field in social reproduction that gives revolutionary struggle a new avenue to push forward, the stronghold of capital today is entirely different to what it once was. Although our tools may still breathe the same oxygen the relationship amongst the fumes we exhale will look different. Gutierrez’s emphasis on the particular is not one that existed in the traditional Marxist-Leninist one, so through it we can revitalize the output of the struggle. The Zapatista liberated territory reshapes itself, and its continuation 25 years later is not just an experiment in autonomy but in verifiable emancipation, in true horizontal organization on a breathable scale. Through emancipation there clears up a road that tasks itself with the actualization of communal will; beyond power and beyond the crushing weight of capital. 



[1] Gutiérrez, Raquel. "The Rhythms of the Pachakuti: Brief Reflections Regarding How We Have Come to Know Emancipatory Struggles and the Significance of the Term Social Emancipation." South Atlantic Quarterly, vol. 111, no. 3, 2012
